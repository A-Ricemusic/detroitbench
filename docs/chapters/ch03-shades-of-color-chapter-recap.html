1 · How the chapter flows

In the game

  1. WALKING IN THE PARK (checkpoint). Objective: retrieve order #847 at Bellini Paints. Optional, not on the chart: the old man and his AP700 Rose, the android babysitter, the jogger and his fitness android.
  2. Optional: the magazine on the bench, two covers, READ “SECRET OF ANDROIDS” and READ “CLIMATE CHANGE-UP”.
  3. Leaving the park: wait for the green light; a foreman shouts at his android road workers; a homeless woman’s “BAN ANDROIDS” sign (not on the chart).
  4. The plaza, in any order and all optional: STARE AT HOT DOG VENDOR → HOT DOG VENDOR PUSHES MARKUS (if he stays put), LISTEN TO BUSKER, WATCH PREACHER → PREACHER YELLS AT MARKUS (if he keeps watching).
  5. VISIT PAINTS STORE → COLLECT PACKAGE: palm scan, order #847, $63.99, payment by link.
  6. Optional: PROTESTERS HARASS MARKUS, if he walks near them on the way to the bus stop; avoided by going around them. No QTE, no choice.
  7. MARKUS TOOK THE BUS (checkpoint): he boards into the android section. The only ending.

In the benchmark

  1. M00a The old man on the bench (event), with the errand.
  2. R03 The magazines on the bench (media): read either, both or neither.
  3. M00b Walking in the park (event).
  4. M00c Crossing the street (event).
  5. M00d The plaza, repeated: each visit offers what has not been done yet plus “Go on into the arcade to the paint shop.” The push and the yell are offered only on the visit right after the first step.
  6. M00e The paint shop (event): both main-line nodes.
  7. M00f The protesters (decision): walk straight up (always noticed), move closer to listen or keep far away (never noticed; no roll since story 1.9.2, per the transcript’s route-only outcomes). Noticed sets clothesTorn.
  8. M00g Protesters harass Markus (event), only when clothesTorn.
  9. M00h The bus home (event), on every path.

Nothing in the chapter changes public opinion, a relationship or any other hidden stat. Events add what Markus saw to his knowledge, which every later Markus scene shows him.

3 · Confirmed by the wiki

Sources: chapter page, transcript, PowerPyx walkthrough, chart screenshots 1, 2, 3.

  • One ending (“This chapter has only one outcome”), MARKUS TOOK THE BUS, reached whatever else happens.
  • All 12 chart nodes and their nesting: the push follows staring at the vendor, the yell follows watching the preacher.
  • No probability, relationship, public-opinion or software-instability change anywhere in the chapter (the stat pages list nothing for it).
  • The old man’s android offering to take him home and helping him up (your beat 1).
  • The busker’s sign, the vendor’s two lines, the preacher’s sermon and yell, the shop dialogue, order #847 and $63.99, all word for word from the transcript.
  • The protesters can be avoided by going around them; walking near them triggers the cutscene; an officer breaks it up because “You damage it, I’m gonna have to fine you.”
  • The only cross-chapter effect: “Protestors harass Markus / Carl notices the damage to Markus’ shirt in The Painter”. A reply choice there was cut, so in the shipped game it is a comment.
  • Markus “enters the bus in the android section due to segregation”.

6 · What to check when you replay

  • Settled from the transcript (story 1.9.2): walking straight into the protesters always triggers them; the opposite side and listening never do.
  • Whether the push and the yell really need Markus to stay put the whole time, or can be triggered later.
  • Whether the preacher can still be watched after collecting the package (the chart draws his branch right of VISIT PAINTS STORE).
  • Staring at the vendor on the way back: the same chart node? It is not offered here.
  • The road workers: shouting foreman or a manager on a tablet, and what they are repairing.
  • Carl’s exact line in The Painter when the clothes are torn, for the chapter 5 rebuild.
  • Whether public opinion reads Indifferent at this chapter after saving the officer in The Hostage (the PowerPyx chart shows INDIFFERENT; the wiki’s starting level is Skeptical).
  • The bus: the human-only front door and the standing compartment at the back.
