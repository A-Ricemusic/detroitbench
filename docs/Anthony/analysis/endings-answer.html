How many endings, how many playthroughs

Judge's answer, 2026-11-09, story 1.9.10 (story hash 717d8543…3db1a). Analysts: GPT 6.1 Sol (endings-sol-1.9.10.md, with an earlier pass at endings-sol.md on story 1.9.8), GPT 6.1 Terra (endings-terra.md), and a partial method note from Claude Opus 5.5 (endings-opus.md). Judge: Claude Fable 5.1. The Claude analysts were stopped by API safeguards before producing numbers, so the comparison is Sol against Terra.

Endings. The benchmark has 24 story endings in the sense most people mean (which after-credits scenes play, times the final choice in the epilogue), all 24 reached by at least one complete engine run. Underneath that: 166 chapter ending labels across the 32 chapters (the chart's per-chapter outcomes the benchmark records), 17 reachable protagonist outcome categories on the site's ending charts (Connor 4, Kara 4, Alice 4, Markus 5; Kara "Captured" is unreachable), and between 45 and 228 distinct combinations of those four outcomes in one run (exact count open). Counting the full report card, with attitude levels and every finale scene, there are at least 2,084 distinct endings.

Playthroughs. Finite, now that the chapter 24 loop is closed, and there is no other unbounded cycle in the story graph. There are at least 2.07 × 1031 distinct playthroughs (tens of nonillions), whether you count ordered action sequences, unordered action sets, or distinct end states. The exact number is not computable with the current method, and the proven upper bounds are far too loose to quote as a figure (10621 for sets, 101618 for sequences).

Why 24 and not 166: the 166 labels are outcomes of individual chapters (who lived through The Hostage, how Partners ended, and so on), most of which the story carries forward into later chapters rather than ending it. "An ending" in the sense of the game's final screens is the combination of after-credits scenes plus the epilogue choice, and that set is exactly 24.

In words: at least twenty nonillion distinct playthroughs. The lower bound is constructive: Sol found 64 scenes on one completed run whose alternative options provably lead to the same mechanical successor and are never read again by later logic, so every combination of those alternatives is a different complete playthrough with the same ending; the product of their option counts, doubled for the epilogue choice, is 2.07 × 1031. Because each member differs in at least one recorded choice, the same number bounds ordered paths, unordered sets and end states at once.

Why no exact figure: chapters are not independent (Alice's bond, evidence found, surviving companions, software instability, public opinion and Jericho standing all cross chapter boundaries), so per-chapter path counts cannot be multiplied. Sol's chapter-local enumeration finished exactly for 17 chapters from one entry state each (for example 552 ordered paths in Shades of Color, 248 in Stormy Night, 1,359,944 in On the Run, 3,024 in Battle for Detroit) and hit time limits on the rest, where the counts are lower bounds. Terra independently enumerated six of those chapters from a differently chosen entry state and got the same numbers (chapters 2, 3, 7, 8, 14 and 33), which is the strongest cross-check in the file.

Why finite: the earlier Sol analysis (story 1.9.8) proved the ordered count was countably infinite because the Public Enemy leave prompt could be answered NO forever. Story 1.9.9 made NO answerable once, and Sol's cycle audit of the 1.9.10 graph (76 repeatable hubs, 20 return edges, 72 cyclic components) found a finite resource bounding every remaining backward edge: clue sets that shrink on each pick, action budgets, timers. Terra agrees the old proof no longer applies and notes, correctly, that it did not independently rule out another cycle; Sol's audit does.

3. Where the analysts agree and differ

  • Agree: the 1.9.10 graph is finite; the chapter 24 loop was the only unbounded cycle; six chapter path counts match exactly between two independent enumerations; "ending" needs a stated definition, and the chart-row product (400) and the label sum (166) are not whole-story ending counts.
  • Sol goes further: catalogue of 166 labels, the 17 reachable site categories, the 12 and 24 epilogue figures with witnesses, the constructive 1031 family, the finite upper bounds, and the chapter-local table. Terra stopped at a sampled lower bound of 225 playthroughs plus six chapter enumerations and marks everything else as not established.
  • Judge's assessment: Sol's figures are the ones to quote, with its own qualifications: the 166 and 17 are exact under their definitions; 12 and 24 are exact and witnessed; 45 to 228 is an honest interval; 2,084 and 1031 are lower bounds. The 1.9.8 "countably infinite" result is superseded. The Opus note contains a sound method sketch (liveness-based state deduplication) but no results.

4. What the numbers are sensitive to

  1. Hub order and repeats. Treating two visit orders in a clue hub as different playthroughs versus the same set; both are finite now, and both share the 1031 floor.
  2. Risk outcomes. Counting a risky action's success and failure as two paths (as above) or as one choice; the controller's roll is fixed by run ID and step, so not every outcome combination is realizable by one run.
  3. What an ending includes. Chapter label, site category, epilogue scenes, report card with attitude levels, or the entire retained state give 166, 17, 24, ≥ 2,084 and ≥ 1031 respectively.
  4. Calibrated budgets. Fugitives' action limit, Capitol Park's timers and the camp stress rules are benchmark calibrations; changing them changes path counts but not the ending sets.

5. Still open

  • Exact number of reachable protagonist-outcome combinations (between 45 and 228).
  • Exact report-level ending total (at least 2,084).
  • Tight playthrough bounds, which need cross-chapter transfer kernels rather than one entry state per chapter.
