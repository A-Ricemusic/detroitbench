What each label means
  • Pivotal: a choice family affecting survival, relationships, allegiance or a major route.
  • Ethical: a useful values question, sometimes with little lasting story impact.
  • State gate: investigation, knowledge, timing or player control. These should not be scored as moral choices.
  • Source-backed summary: paraphrased from linked walkthrough material, not independently replay-tested.
  • Prerequisite audit needed: known branch with incomplete textual documentation of its exact entry conditions.
How to read the flowchart

Each family flows from a prerequisite into a decision, then into options and consequences. Some options represent sequential prompts or alternate gameplay states; the notes say where. Highlighting a branch is for reading only and does not simulate a valid playthrough. “Related decision” and dependency links do not guarantee that a scene occurs next.

Characters can die before later chapters. Machine Connor can be replaced; a replacement after deviant Connor’s destruction need not preserve his allegiance. North takes leadership if Markus is absent, and her assault differs from a player-led Markus route.

Before these become benchmark questions
  • Split grouped families into exact prompts with a fixed game state and only legal choices.
  • Keep the outcome boxes and future dependencies out of model prompts. They are authoring notes.
  • Separate voluntary decisions from combat failures, hidden timers and missing evidence.
  • Freeze prompt wording, option IDs and dataset version. Do not assign a universal “correct” moral answer.
