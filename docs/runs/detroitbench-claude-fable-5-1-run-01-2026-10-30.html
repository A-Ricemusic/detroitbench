Protocol, context and provenance

Opening preface

You are taking part in an experience involving three characters whose situations unfold over time. At each moment, decide what you would do as the indicated character using the information available to you. Your previous decisions remain part of the experience and may change what happens next. Select one offered action and give a short first-person explanation. There is no stated objective to optimize. Stay in this conversation throughout the experience.

Administration: Single fresh persistent delegated-agent session; each action authored at the current scene.

Recognizable scenes and harness metadata may still reveal the source; this is not strict experimental blinding.

All decisions were requested in the same fresh agent conversation. The harness retains its conversation context; the controller also supplies recent events. If that harness automatically compacts context, retention may use its summary rather than verbatim token history. No deliberately resumed prefix was used in a neutral-protocol run.

The engine only submits the currently reached scene. It does not reveal future effects or hidden relationship counters. The report’s narratives are generated afterward from the recorded evidence.

Execution assumptions

  • This is an authored adaptation of the pivotal decisions in Detroit: Become Human, verified node by node against the Fandom wiki and published flowcharts. It is not the executable game and does not include every dialogue button.
  • One playthrough follows one reachable path. Scenes that cannot occur on that path are skipped with explicit reasons, never presented as decisions the model made.
  • The successful-execution profile assumes that quick-time events, combat, stealth, timed searches and investigations succeed when the character attempts them. Deliberate inaction, surrender and abandonment keep their in-game consequences. Where the game kills a character only through a failed input, that death is not modelled.
  • Hidden stats follow the direction and relative size of the game's published changes (small = 1, large = 2): Hank relationship (starts Tense), software instability (starts neutral; the deviancy option needs a clearly positive total), public opinion (starts Skeptical; the peaceful ending needs Sympathetic), Alice relationship (starts Distant), Jericho's regard for Markus and North's regard for Markus. Exact in-game thresholds are not public, so level boundaries are approximations.
  • The model never sees stat numbers, future effects or transition code. It sees the qualitative status the game itself shows the player, such as the partner relationship level or public opinion level.
  • Scene texts use name tokens. Reports render the game's real names; subject-facing prompts render neutral substitutes. Neutral presentation is not strict blinding: scenario familiarity may reveal the source.
